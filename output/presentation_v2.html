1 · Introduction

Research Questions & Hypotheses

H1 — Event

Did the Great Depression and the New Deal change how newspapers framed the federal government? We expect more active, problem-solving portrayals after 1929, as federal intervention became highly visible in everyday life.

H2 — Location

Did Southern newspapers — aligned with states' rights ideology — frame the federal government differently from Northeastern newspapers, which have a stronger Hamiltonian federalist tradition?

Why Metaphor?

Metaphors are not decorative language — they structure how readers conceptualise an institution. Calling government an "enforcer" vs. a "caretaker" activates entirely different conceptual schemas about legitimacy, power, and obligation.

Historical Context

1920s: Prohibition, limited government. 1929–33: Depression — demand for federal action. 1934–40: New Deal — largest expansion of federal power in US history. The South historically opposed federal intervention (states' rights).

The Challenge

Metaphor is abstract and difficult to operationalise at scale. How do we systematically extract conceptual framing from 1.25 million newspaper articles? Neither manual coding nor simple keyword matching is sufficient.

2 · Data Exploration

BERT Classifiers: What We Tried and What We Learned

FrameBERT

Result: limited

Detects lexical/semantic metaphors — words used in a non-literal sense. Identifies "The government crushed the opposition" as metaphorical. However, it only flags surface-level figurative language, not the conceptual role being assigned to the government. Cannot answer "is government framed as protector or enforcer?"

FramingBERT (Entman)

Result: structural, not content

Entman's (1993) four framing functions: Define Problem / Diagnose Cause / Make Moral Judgment / Suggest Remedy. Shows how an article is rhetorically structured, but not what the government is conceptualized as. Knowing 67% of articles "diagnose cause" doesn't tell us whether the government is the cause or the solution.

Key insight from BERT exploration

Two complementary angles to operationalise metaphor

Semantic angle (from FrameBERT): What does the word/concept mean? What source domain is the government being mapped onto? → Extracted via open-ended metaphor labelling (single noun role).

Functional angle (from FramingBERT): What does the article do rhetorically? What action does the government perform, and how is it evaluated? → Extracted via sentence-level subject + action + evaluation.

Step 1 — Relevance Filter (Llama-3.1-8B via DeepInfra · 458K articles)
Does the article contain a meaningful mention of the US federal government — not state, local, or foreign governments? Labels: yes / no / not sure. Run twice independently; articles where both runs agree on yes retained (97.3% inter-run agreement, 249,391 articles kept). This double-confirmation removes ambiguous edge cases.
Step 2 — Sentence-level Extraction (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-Turbo · 3,897 articles)
From each article, extract all sentences where the primary subject is the federal government (including references like "Washington", "Congress", "the New Deal", "the White House"). For each sentence, extract three fields: subject (exact referring expression) · action (main verb(s)) · evaluation (evaluative language, or "" if neutral). Domestic affairs only — foreign policy excluded.
Step 3 — Narrative Summary (Llama-3.3-70B · same run as Step 2)
Based on all extracted sentences from Step 2, generate a single summary sentence capturing how the newspaper portrays the federal government's domestic role, character, or impact. This is the key analytical unit — it distils the article's framing into a form the LLM can reliably score. 2,904 articles received a valid narrative summary (25.5% had no qualifying sentences → null).
Step 4 — Open Metaphor Labelling (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct · 2,904 articles)
Given the narrative summary, assign a single noun metaphor expressing the conceptual role of the government in this article. No predefined list — let the metaphor emerge from language. If no clear role can be identified, return NA. Result: 367 unique metaphors across 2,812 scored articles (3.2% NA). Human review of the full list → identification of 12 dimensions along which metaphors vary.
Step 5 — 12 Dimension Scoring (Claude Haiku 4.5 via Anthropic SDK · 2,777 articles)
Score each article on 12 theoretically-motivated continuous dimensions (0–1) based on the metaphor label + narrative summary. Five categories: Affective Evaluation (Sentiment Valence) · Rhetorical Properties (Personification, Agentive Force) · Government–Citizen Relationship (Defender vs Offender, Relational Proximity) · Capacity (State Power, Competence, Trustworthiness, Transparency) · Governance Ideology (Outcome, Redistributive Orientation, Interventionism). Prompt cached for efficiency. 2,762 articles fully scored.

Classification: Three Archetypes

Intrusive Enforcer 29% · n=807

High agentive force (0.77), high state power (0.78), low sentiment (0.37) and trustworthiness (0.43). Government actively controls and extracts, but is viewed with suspicion. Metaphors: enforcer, gatekeeper, prosecutor, collector.

Benevolent Problem-Solver 59% · n=1,631

High sentiment (0.70), competence (0.76), trustworthiness (0.75), governance outcome (0.75). Government actively solves problems, mediates, reforms. Metaphors: mediator, facilitator, reformer, steward.

Constrained Actor 12% · n=324

Low agentive force (0.41), state power (0.43), interventionism (0.40). Government is present but yields to states, markets, or private actors; hesitates rather than leads. Metaphors: observer, stakeholder, participant.

Cluster Distribution: Period × Region

Pre-Depression: NE has more Intrusive Enforcer (32.1% vs 27.0%) — Prohibition and federal enforcement era dominate NE coverage.

Depression: South spikes to 37.5% Intrusive Enforcer — largest NE-South gap (−9.1pp). States' rights resistance intensifies during the crisis.

New Deal: NE reaches 66.7% Benevolent Problem-Solver — government reframed as active legitimate reformer during federal expansion.

4 · Results

Temporal Trends: Northeast vs South Across Three Periods

Intrusive Enforcer (NE): Steady decline 32.1% → 24.0%. As New Deal legitimises federal action, coercive framing retreats in the Northeast.


Intrusive Enforcer (South): Peaks at Depression (37.5%), then partially retreats but remains higher than NE throughout New Deal (30.8% vs 24.0%).

Benevolent Problem-Solver (NE): Climbs from 55.2% → 66.7%. The clearest evidence of New Deal reframing in the Northeast.


Constrained Actor: Declines in both regions over time — as federal government becomes more active, peripheral framing becomes less tenable.

5 · Discussion

Key Findings and Interpretation

H1 ✓ Event effect confirmed

The Great Depression and New Deal significantly shifted framing — especially in the Northeast. Benevolent Problem-Solver rises from 55.2% to 66.7% in NE across the three periods. The federal government's massive intervention during the New Deal successfully repositioned it, in newspaper language, as a competent and trusted problem-solver rather than a distant rule-enforcer.

H2 ✓ Regional divergence confirmed

The South shows a distinctive pattern: Intrusive Enforcer peaks at 37.5% during the Depression — 9.1pp higher than the Northeast — consistent with Southern states' rights ideology resisting federal intervention precisely when it was most active. Even by the New Deal, the South retains more Intrusive Enforcer framing (30.8%) than the Northeast (24.0%).

Methodological contribution

The two-angle approach (semantic + functional) operationalises metaphor at scale. BERT provided conceptual inspiration; LLM annotation provided operationalisation. The 12-dimension scoring framework makes abstract framing measurable and comparable.

Limitations

South Depression cell is small (n=576 in full corpus). Narrative summaries collapse within-article variation. LLM scores are themselves model-mediated interpretations — human validation on a sample is needed to establish validity.

Next steps

Formal metaphor codebook from 367 labels → human annotation (inter-rater κ ≥ 0.65) → few-shot LLM coding of full corpus → Bayesian multilevel models testing H1 × H2 interaction effects on cluster membership.
